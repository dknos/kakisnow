# Visual and technical decisions

Each entry records a deliberate departure or clarification against the
implementation brief.

| Decision | Rationale |
| --- | --- |
| Adapt the strongest available MIT-licensed renderer foundation, retain its exact copyright and license notice, and brand the product KAKISNOW. | The imported renderer provides the strongest technical and visual foundation while the shipped product maintains one cohesive identity. |
| Ship WebGPU only, with a single explanatory stop when `navigator.gpu` is unavailable. | The target is current Windows Chrome on a discrete GPU; WebGL, mobile, and feature-downgrade paths are outside the brief. |
| Use an eight-ring, one-draw geometry clipmap with 8.5 cm inner spacing, roughly 870 m reach, and approximately 333,000 terrain triangles. | This exceeds the requested near-field density while preserving a continuous horizon at low draw-call cost. |
| Bake the procedural 4096² terrain heightfield on the GPU before the loading screen dismisses. | Broad dunes, medium wind forms, and directional fine ridges are stable in world space and do not consume per-frame noise evaluation. |
| Use a 2048² RGBA16F ping-pong state field over 80 m instead of the suggested 4096² R16F starting point. | Approximately 3.9 cm texels retain convincing trail edges and displaced mass at substantially lower persistent render-target cost. |
| Pack depression, displaced mass, compression, and ice into the terrain state; omit a separate wetness channel. | The current spell-water response is transient, while compression and ice provide the persistent shading states that materially affect the captured frame. |
| Scroll the deformation window in snapped world-space texel increments and accumulate brush splats through a GPU simulation pass. | This keeps persistent marks stable under player movement without rebuilding them from event history. |
| Use three custom WGSL PCSS-style shadow cascades rather than Babylon's stock cascaded-shadow material path. | Terrain, procedural heroes, RockerKaki, spell geometry, and deformation can share the same raw-WGSL lighting conventions and pipeline warm-up. |
| Generate the distant mountain field procedurally in the custom sky rather than render the retained mountain paintings. | The procedural ridgeline holds up around unrestricted camera orbit and integrates directly with aerial perspective; the older mattes remain vendored only as archival assets. |
| Keep the retained Poly Haven HDRI and Snow 02 maps archival rather than sample them in the current renderer. | The active custom sky and procedural snow surface are more controllable and avoid runtime texture dependency; the CC0 sources remain documented and available for future art studies. |
| Make RockerKaki the default playable hero and retain Snowbound as the selectable articulated variant. | The project-owner character gives the public demo its identity; the shared controller, surf lean, spell handoff, and snow contact keep it fully playable without fabricating a weak skeletal walk cycle. |
| Give RockerKaki a native custom beauty/depth/shadow treatment rather than a stock PBR material. | Shared sun, cascades, fog, spell light, and snow contact make the authored model belong to the scene. |
| Use preallocated swept meshes and pooled particles rather than full screen-space fluid rendering. | Coherent silhouettes, refraction cues, spray, and persistent snow writes produce more useful pixels within the frame budget. |
| Keep Ribbon as a held stream while driving its tip through a camera-relative, momentum-smoothed figure-eight. | It preserves the requested continuous control while remaining legible without a crosshair; release throws and retires the existing body instead of despawning it. |
| Represent Vortex surface stripping through its shared deformation mark and timed refill rather than maintain a separate airborne-mass reservoir. | The visible ground-to-air-to-ground handoff remains readable without another persistent simulation volume. |
| Warm materials, render targets, shadows, post stages, hero variants, and all five spell pipelines before fade-in. | A longer intentional load is preferable to first-use WebGPU pipeline compilation hitches. |
| Keep runtime captures and profiles explicit about their hardware and measurement limits. | Headless or audit-machine data demonstrates regressions and hitch behavior but cannot certify the specified RTX 5070 Ti target by itself. |
| Keep RockerKaki available locally but gate commercial redistribution. | Its AI-generation lineage is documented, but the remove.bg account tier in the source chain cannot currently be verified. |
| Keep Summit Line as a local feature layer over the original rolling GPU terrain. | Three crest-aligned jump lips and two locally shaped halfpipes retain the natural field everywhere else while sharing exact geometry with CPU grounding, lighting, shadows, camera clearance, deformation, and spell impacts. |
| Give the shared controller one grounded/airborne ballistic state instead of faking a jump in either hero adapter. | Space input, coyote time, buffered takeoff, ramp release, landing impact, snow contact, camera motion, the procedural figure, and RockerKaki all read the same authoritative jump. |
| Keep the reproducible Blender rig embedded, but use rigid runtime poses for RockerKaki. | The source is 1,901 disconnected surface islands, so spatial bone deformation stretches its face, hair, and guitar. Holding the nine deform bones in their authored pose while animating the complete model preserves the exact silhouette and keeps color, depth, and shadows synchronized. |
| Put the board between the motion root and the rider rather than beside her. | The rider is a passenger. Solving the board's attitude first and hanging her off it makes her tip with the edge by construction; the alternative is two nodes reading the same terrain and disagreeing about it by a frame. |
| Derive the surf trench from the board's measured waist and effective edge instead of from tuned constants. | `src/character/boardSpec.js` is the one record of the mesh's geometry, so the groove is the board's own footprint. A board is a visible object now, and a trench that is not its size and heading reads as a decal sliding underneath it. |
| Convert the controller's heading into the deformation brush's angle convention at the call site rather than change the shader. | `brush()` orients its long axis along `(cos yaw, sin yaw)`; the controller's forward is `(sin f, cos f)`. Ribbon's travel direction and Vortex's ring tangent are both authored in the shader's convention and are correct against it, and Bloom's and Crystallize's ejecta take a random or golden-angle heading that reads the same either way — so changing the shader would have broken two correct call sites to fix the wrong one. `snowContact.js` had elongated every footprint and the whole surf groove across the direction of travel at the Summit Line heading. `tests/brush-yaw.test.mjs` holds the two conventions together. |
| Leave the same convention error standing in `sweep.js` rather than fix it alongside the board. | Sweep's `Math.atan2(rz, -rx)` is written against the controller's convention like `snowContact.js` was, so its trench is elongated almost radially where its own comment says the yaw is the arc tangent — measured dot with the radial is -0.90 against 0 for a true tangent. The correction is one line, `Math.atan2(rx, -rz)`, but it changes a spell's mark on the snow and belongs to a change about spells, not to a change about a board. |
| Let the board sink well short of the trench floor it is cutting. | The deformation pass keeps digging for as long as a brush is over a texel and only stops at its 0.55 m clamp, so matching the floor would bury the board the moment the player held a line. Riding high in its own groove with the berms standing either side is what reads as displaced snow. Sink scales with `S.deformDepth` so the overlay slider moves the board with the surface. |
| Recover part of the board's attitude on the rider instead of fixing her perpendicular to it. | The demo opens on a 43-degree face. A rider held square to a board on that slope reclines into the hill with her weight behind the tail; a real one stands nearer vertical and only commits to the board's plane through a turn. Roll recovers less than pitch, because leaning into a carve is something riders actually do. |
| Lift the board above the CPU height mirror by the sastrugi crest height. | `heightAt` reconstructs the macro heightfield only; `snow.vertex.wgsl` then displaces every vertex again by `terrainFine`, whose crests stand about 8 cm proud and which is never read back. A 2.58 m character hid that gap for the whole life of the project. A 7.6 cm board is thinner than the relief it sits in, so it was swallowed whole. The effective edge is 2.4 m against a 2.3 m sastrugi wavelength, so a stiff board spans a full ridge period and rests on the crests — lifting by the crest height is the physical answer rather than a fudge, and it scales with `S.sastrugiStrength` so turning the sastrugi off settles the board back onto the macro surface. |
| Measure the board's sink in deck heights rather than in metres. | What the eye judges is how much board is left above the snow, which is the deck minus the sink. A fixed 4.5 cm idle sink swallowed three quarters of a 6 cm deck whenever the player slowed down, and would not have tracked `S.boardScale` — a bigger board would have been buried by exactly as much. |
| Make the board's size a single scale on one authored length, and the trench derive from the same proportions. | Resizing a mesh without resizing its groove puts a bigger board in a smaller board's track, which is the failure `boardSpec.js` exists to prevent. Holding one length and a set of measured ratios means the elongation of the contact brush is scale-invariant and only its radius has to move. |
| Offer the board as a toggle rather than assuming every player wants it. | RockerKaki was authored seated and rode nothing for the whole life of the project before this. Switching the board off restores that: the mesh goes, she sits at snow level, and her contact reverts to a broad seated scuff rather than leaving a snowboard track behind a character visibly not on one. Terrain conformance stays either way, because that was never the board's doing. |
| Centre RockerKaki on the board by vertex centroid rather than by bounding box. | A bounding box is set by whatever reaches furthest, and what reaches furthest on this model is the guitar held out to her left — so centring the box centred the guitar and pushed her body 19 cm right and 8 cm forward of the board's centreline. The centroid is the body, because the body is where the geometry is, and it still accounts for the guitar's own mass. Measured 0.3 mm lateral against the board on flat ground afterwards. Y stays on the bounding box: she has to stand on her lowest point, not her average one. |
| Guard the controller's acceleration divide against a zero timestep. | `S.freezeTime` feeds a dt of exactly zero, so the quotient was 0/0. Acceleration went NaN, lean and carve inherited it, and every consumer downstream got a NaN rotation. It had cost only a slightly wrong body lean while nothing else read those values; it now costs the board's entire attitude, so the latent bug became a visible one. Holding the last value is what "frozen" should mean regardless. |
